Karnataka at a glance

Karnataka sits in south-western India, between the Arabian Sea and the Deccan plateau. It was formed on 1 November 1956 by joining Kannada-speaking regions, and the day is celebrated every year as Kannada Rajyotsava.

The state has three landscapes: the green Western Ghats, the sandy coastal belt and the dry plateau of the north. Because of this, one trip can include a beach, a coffee estate and a 1,000-year-old temple.

Tourist places

Five places that show the different sides of Karnataka.

Mysuru

Home of the Mysore Palace, lit by about 100,000 bulbs on Sundays and festival days. Famous for the Dasara celebrations, silk sarees and sandalwood.

Bengaluru

The capital and India's tech hub. Visit Lalbagh Botanical Garden, Cubbon Park and Vidhana Soudha, then try the cafes and street food.

Coorg (Kodagu)

Misty hills, coffee and pepper estates, and a warrior culture of its own. Known for homestays and Coorg pandi curry.

Gokarna

A temple town with quiet beaches such as Om Beach and Kudle Beach. A calmer alternative to bigger beach destinations.

Udupi and Malpe

Udupi is known for the Sri Krishna Matha temple and its vegetarian kitchens. Malpe beach is nearby, with boat rides to St Mary's Island.

Historical places

Karnataka was ruled by the Chalukyas, Hoysalas, Vijayanagara kings, Adil Shahis and Wodeyars. Each left buildings you can still visit. Read the list below from oldest to newest style.

  1. Badami and Pattadakal Chalukya, 6th to 8th century

    Rock-cut cave temples above a lake at Badami, and a group of temples at Pattadakal that mixes northern and southern styles. Pattadakal is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

  2. Shravanabelagola Ganga dynasty, 10th century

    A 57-foot monolithic statue of Gommateshwara (Bahubali) stands on a hill. The Mahamastakabhisheka ritual happens once in about 12 years.

  3. Belur and Halebidu Hoysala, 12th and 13th century

    Temples covered in fine stone carving, from tiny dancers to whole epics. Added to the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2023.

  4. Hampi Vijayanagara Empire, 14th to 16th century

    The ruined capital of a great empire, spread across boulders beside the Tungabhadra river. See the Stone Chariot, Virupaksha Temple and Lotus Mahal. UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986.

  5. Gol Gumbaz, Vijayapura Adil Shahi, 17th century

    A huge domed tomb where a whisper can be heard many times over in the Whispering Gallery.

  6. Srirangapatna Tipu Sultan, 18th century

    An island fort in the Cauvery river with Tipu's Summer Palace and the Daria Daulat Bagh.

Waterfalls and hill stations

Go in or just after the monsoon (June to September) for full waterfalls, and in winter for clear hill views.

Waterfalls

  • Jog Falls: the Sharavathi river drops about 250 m in four streams named Raja, Rani, Roarer and Rocket.
  • Shivanasamudra: the Cauvery splits into Gaganachukki and Bharachukki, close to Mysuru.
  • Abbey Falls: a waterfall inside a coffee estate near Madikeri.
  • Iruppu Falls: a sacred waterfall on the Coorg and Kerala border.
  • Magod Falls: a two-step fall near Yellapur in Uttara Kannada.

Hill stations

  • Chikmagalur: coffee country and Mullayanagiri, the highest peak in Karnataka.
  • Madikeri (Coorg): Raja's Seat viewpoint and misty mornings.
  • Nandi Hills: an easy sunrise trip from Bengaluru.
  • Agumbe: rainforest village known for rain and sunsets. Called the "Cherrapunji of the South".
  • Kudremukh: grass-covered peaks and a national park.

Famous foods

Hover over a card (or tap it on a phone) to see where to find the dish.

Masala Dosa

Crisp rice crepe with potato filling. Try the Benne Dose of Davangere.

Bisi Bele Bath

Spicy rice, lentils and vegetables cooked together. Best served hot with boondi.

Mysore Pak

A gram flour and ghee sweet first made in the Mysore Palace kitchens.

Ragi Mudde

Finger millet balls swallowed, not chewed, with saaru or soppina curry. Popular in the Mysuru-Mandya belt.

Jolada Rotti Oota

Sorghum flatbread with ennegayi (stuffed brinjal) and chutneys from North Karnataka.

Neer Dosa

Thin, lacy rice dosa from the coast, eaten with coconut chutney or chicken curry.

Dharwad Peda

A brown milk sweet from Dharwad with a caramelised taste.

Filter Coffee

Strong decoction with hot milk, served in a steel tumbler and davara.

Getting around

  • By air: Kempegowda International Airport (Bengaluru), plus airports at Mangaluru, Hubballi and Mysuru.
  • By train: Bengaluru, Mysuru, Hubballi and Mangaluru are the main railway hubs.
  • By bus: KSRTC and private buses connect almost every district.
  • In Bengaluru: Namma Metro is the fastest way to avoid traffic.

Tips before you go

What should I pack?

Light cotton clothes for the plains and coast, a rain jacket for the Ghats in monsoon, and comfortable shoes for temples and treks.

Any temple rules?

Dress modestly, remove footwear before entering, and check whether photography is allowed inside.

Is it safe to visit waterfalls in monsoon?

Stay behind railings, avoid slippery rocks and follow local forest department advice. Some viewpoints close during heavy rain.

Can I book everything online?

Trains, buses and most hotels can be booked online. For small homestays in places like Coorg, call the owner first.
